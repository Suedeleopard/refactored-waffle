# Content pipeline

The site is a shell until there are ten or so teardowns on it. This file is the
backlog so you never sit down to a blank page.

## The three kinds of post

Keep these separate. Blurring them is the one thing that costs you authority.

| Kind | Whose idea | What it buys you | Frequency |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Found** | Someone else's. A real business already behaving this way. | Credibility. Proof the pattern exists in the wild and isn't a thing you made up. Easiest to produce — research, not invention. | Every other week |
| **Made** | Yours. Hypothetical, generated by running the method. | Desire for the method. This is what makes people want The Kit. Ends with "your turn". | Every other week |
| **Shipped** | Yours, and it ran. Real client, real budget, real result. | Everything. The only kind that proves the method survives contact with a client. | Whenever you can |

You have at least one Shipped already: **NYDG Foundation** — a sun cream brand
behaving like a charity, and a charity behaving like a fashion label. That is
your single strongest asset and it isn't on the site yet.

The rare fourth kind is **Aimed**: a Made teardown pointed at a specific named
business you'd like to work with. It's outreach disguised as content. Use it
sparingly, maybe one in eight, and only for businesses you'd genuinely take on.

## Made — yours to invent

Run the method properly on each. The Y is chosen because the audience already
spends money there, not because the pairing sounds clever.

| The swap | Why the audience overlaps |
|---|---|
| A dentist behaving like a luxury hotel | Private dental patients already pay for good hotels. Both sell a stretch of time you'd rather not think about. |
| An accountant behaving like a personal trainer | Both are "someone who keeps you honest about numbers you avoid". Monthly check-ins, visible progress, plans. |
| A funeral director behaving like a wedding planner | Same family, same once-in-a-lifetime logistics, wildly different levels of care and design attention. |
| An estate agent behaving like a travel influencer | People shop for houses the way they shop for holidays, and one of those industries got very good at making you want to be somewhere. |
| A law firm behaving like a gym | Membership rather than the billable hour. Check-ins, plans, and a relationship instead of a crisis. |
| A plumber behaving like an F1 pit crew | Speed, choreography, kit laid out, timed and visible. A category where nobody has ever made competence into a spectacle. |
| An independent bookshop behaving like a streaming service | Curation, "if you liked this", staff as tastemakers with followings, a queue of what's next. |
| A butcher behaving like a sommelier | Pairing, provenance, tasting notes and vintages. Wine solved the language problem meat never bothered with. |
| A garden centre behaving like an art gallery | Both sell slow-growing objects for the home to people with taste and patience. One of them has openings and editions. |
| An optician behaving like a jeweller | Glasses are the only jewellery most people wear every single day, sold like a medical appliance. |
| A recruiter behaving like a football scout | Scouting reports, watchlists, development paths, transfer windows. Recruitment already is this and refuses to admit it. |
| A car dealership behaving like an art gallery | Private views, one object under good light, a catalogue, an appointment. Not a forecourt and a lanyard. |
| A removals firm behaving like a concierge | The most stressful week of someone's year, handled by people trained only to lift. |
| A vet behaving like a children's TV channel | The animal isn't the customer. The frightened child in the waiting room is, and nobody designs for them. |
| A coffee shop behaving like a library | Silence as a feature, membership, a reason to stay four hours instead of forty minutes. |
| A pub behaving like a theatre | Seasons, runs, a programme, and a reason to book rather than wander in. |
| A barber behaving like a private members' club | Standing appointments, a bar, an address people mention. The relationship is already monthly and lifelong. |
| A nursery behaving like a museum | Exhibitions of what the children made, curated and documented, for parents who want to see the learning not just the photos. |
| A physio behaving like a recording studio | Sessions, takes, playback, a produced result. Rehab is already reps and review and nobody has made it feel like craft. |
| A hotel behaving like an estate agent | Selling the life in the area rather than the room. From your own framework doc. |

## The publishing loop

1. **Write the teardown** into `src/lib/teardowns.ts`. One array entry. Ten
   minutes once you have the ideas.
2. **Cut the social post from it.** Ideas only, no reasoning. The reasoning is
   the reason to click.
3. **Post it.** Ends with "your turn, leave yours in the comments".
4. **Write the email** later that week: the working out, plus the best replies
   from the comments, credited by name.
5. **Repeat.** The compounding is the whole strategy.
